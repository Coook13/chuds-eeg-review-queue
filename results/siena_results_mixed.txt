READ WITH CARE
- Same 14 patients / 47 seizures as the first round: one seizure is about 2 points of sensitivity, and each cell is a single run (one seed).
- "children only" here uses the same sampled training rows as the mixed model. It catches 4/47 at th 0.8 (v1) against 5/47 in the first round; that 1-seizure gap is sampling noise.
- Mixed pool: every recording contributes all seizure windows plus up to 1000 other windows; src.detector.fit then keeps 30000 non-seizure windows. Siena is roughly 20% of the seizure windows in the pool.
- v2 compares each window with the recording's own previous 10 minutes. The Siena recordings are trimmed around the seizure (the first seizure sits 13 to 645 min into the file, median 120 min, and 2 files have it within 15 min), so the background is calmer and shorter than a continuous multi-day stay. False alerts per 24 h are probably optimistic for real monitoring, and v2's gain may be partly this.
- False alerts are concentrated: at th 0.8 with v2 mixed, 118 of 130 alerts come from four patients (PN16 51, PN07 38, PN03 15, PN14 14).
- v2 helps the general models a lot (children-only 4/47 -> 25/47 at th 0.8) but not the personal model trained on one or two earlier recordings (13/31 -> 10/31). So the tiny-training-set personal model is not the one that gains from v2.
- Not checked: whether v2 gives the same gain on CHB-MIT itself, so the cause (domain shift removed by the baseline) is a hypothesis consistent with the 1.4 to 2.9 log-power offset found earlier, not a proven mechanism.
