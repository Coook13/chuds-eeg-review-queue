READ WITH CARE
- 14 patients / 47 seizures: small. One seizure is 2 points of sensitivity; per-patient numbers are 1 to 10 seizures each.
- Every one of the 41 recordings contains a seizure (Siena has no seizure-free recordings), so false alerts per 24 h are measured
  on 141.0 h of EEG that were recorded around seizures, not on random days.
- 6 of 47 seizures last 20 s or less (shortest 5 s, as listed). The event logic needs 5 smoothed windows in a row, so these are close to undetectable by design.
- Seizure times come from hand-typed clock times. Fixes applied (data/siena/parse_notes.txt): PN14-3 list start hour is a typo
  (EDF header 19.17.45), PN05-3 list start is 10 s off the header, PN00-3 seizure end 19.29.29 treated as 18.29.29, file-name typos,
  PN10 'clinical onset' time used where two onsets are given. Clock times are only second-accurate, so delays have a few seconds of slack.
- Training on CHB-MIT: 186 of 188 downloaded recordings had cached features (2 skipped). Siena's feature distribution differs a lot from CHB-MIT
  (median band log-power 1.4 to 2.9 lower, Hjorth mobility about 2x higher), so (a) is a real out-of-distribution test, not just a new patient.
- (b) can only test 27 of 41 recordings (31 of 47 seizures): the first seizure recording of each patient has no earlier data.
  (a) and (b) therefore have different denominators; use the 'same recordings' reference block to compare them.
- Training sets in (b) are tiny (often one earlier recording with one seizure), so (b) numbers are high-variance.
- Hybrid (c) is an extra, not requested.
